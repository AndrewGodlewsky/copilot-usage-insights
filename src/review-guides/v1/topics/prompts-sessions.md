Prompts/sessions: character length does not measure detail or completeness; attachments, injected context and conversation history may carry requirements. Excerpts can support observations about visible goals, constraints or acceptance checks. One experiment is adding an acceptance criterion on comparable tasks and comparing clarification and resources without sacrificing quality.

Depth counts confirmed human submissions, excluding sessions with uncertain identity or absent metadata. Buckets and median describe selected dates, not lifetime depth. Multi-day use is calendar activity, not continuous work. Earlier activity proves prior use; its absence does not prove a new session. Do not infer creation rates.

The joint work evidence can suggest autonomous execution (one prompt, many calls), interactive exchanges (many prompts, few calls each), or repeated agent activity (many prompts and calls). These are hypotheses: calls measure activity, not task duration, task count, difficulty or success. Verification, retries and recovery also cause calls. Short text can initiate large tasks; averages hide mixed workloads. The grid requires complete, finished accounting for every counted prompt; do not generalize it to excluded sessions.

Describe the numbers, alternatives and coverage. Sparse or limited evidence merits low confidence. Optional excerpts can test a narrow hypothesis, not reconstruct a workflow. Neither short nor long sessions are inherently efficient; do not recommend longer chats or arbitrary resets from depth alone.
