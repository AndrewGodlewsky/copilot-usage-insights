Prompt length does not measure completeness: attachments, history and injected context may carry requirements. Excerpts can reveal visible goals, constraints or acceptance checks. An experiment is adding an acceptance criterion to comparable tasks and checking clarification, resources and quality.

Depth counts confirmed human submissions in selected dates, not lifetime depth. Uncertain identity and absent metadata are excluded. Multi-day use is not continuous work; missing earlier activity does not prove a new session. Joint prompt/call counts can suggest autonomy or iteration, not task duration, task count, difficulty or success. Retries and verification also cause calls. That grid requires complete finished accounting for every prompt. Do not generalize to excluded sessions, rate short/long sessions, or recommend arbitrary resets.

Delivery is timing: idle starts work, steering joins running work, queued waits. Unknown stays unknown. Session steering rates require every prompt's delivery known. Compare only like coverage. Repeated steering may reflect correction, new information, changed scope or deliberate collaboration. One or two cases cannot establish a habit. Do not diagnose incomplete prompts or give a negative assessment from frequency alone. Existing bounded excerpts and candidate delivery labels can test a specific hypothesis; samples are selective, and missing excerpts cannot confirm it.

User stops are separate explicit local-user aborts, not prompts; they need not be caused by a later message. Other abort reasons are excluded. Missing records do not prove no stops. Never label associated spending wasted or infer causation from timing. State alternatives and coverage; sparse evidence warrants low confidence.
